Rules already in place

Newsrooms and publishers can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules and standards a newsroom or publisher can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. Copyright registration. The U.S. Copyright Office's 2023 guidance says AI-generated content that is more than de minimis should be excluded from a registration claim, so a publisher needs to know which parts a person made.
  2. Advertising. Section 5 of the FTC Act bars deceptive practices, and the FTC's 2015 guide on native advertising applies that to paid content dressed as editorial.
  3. Content Credentials. The C2PA technical specification, a voluntary standard now at version 2.5, lets a checker validate a signed provenance record attached to a file.
  1. Newsroom standards. AP's July 2026 standards update says AI-generated output is reviewed and edited by AP journalists before publication.
  2. If your work reaches Europe. The EU AI Act applies to providers and deployers outside the EU when the output is used in the EU. Article 50 has providers tell people when they are dealing with an AI system and mark AI-generated content.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before you license a tool. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before content goes to production. See the sample requirement.
  2. At acceptance. Sample the tool's real output for source traceability, citations and corrections, against what the vendor promised.
  3. Provenance is not accuracy. A valid signed record shows where a file came from. Whether the content is accurate is a separate question, and it needs its own evidence.
