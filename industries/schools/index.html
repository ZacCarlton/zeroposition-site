Rules already in place

School districts and colleges can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules a district, college or school board can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. Student records. Under FERPA, a school that shares education records with an outside company under the school-official exception must keep direct control over how the company uses and keeps those records.
  2. Children's data. Under the FTC's amended COPPA rule, an online service that collects data from children under 13 must keep a written security program, test its safeguards, and get written assurances before passing children's data to other companies. Most amended requirements apply from April 22, 2026.
  3. Student data in New York. New York's Education Law 2-d and Part 121, in force since January 2020, set student data privacy and security rules for districts and their contractors, including protections written into contracts.
  1. Federal guidance on buying. The U.S. Department of Education's October 2024 toolkit for education leaders advises districts to ask vendors for strong evidence that a product works, such as independent research.
  2. Colorado. Colorado's SB26-189, signed in May 2026, sets documentation, notice and review duties for developers and users of automated decision tools used in decisions about access to education, starting January 1, 2027.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before you approve or renew. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before the board votes. See the sample requirement.
  2. Before student data goes in. Check that the actual setup matches what the vendor said about training on student data, other companies that touch it, how long it is kept and how it is deleted.
  3. When the product changes. A new model or a new contract is a good time for a second look at the claims you rely on.

The claim

"Students who use our tutor gain a full grade level in one semester."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Not enough to judge. The vendor's study compared students who chose to use the tutor with students who didn't, so the gain can't be separated from who signed up.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: None found. The study design can't show the claim is true or false.

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
