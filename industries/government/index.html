Rules already in place

Government buyers can already ask for the evidence

Federal rules and several states already expect an agency to test or check an AI product before it relies on it. These are the rules a purchasing office can point to.

  1. Federal agencies using AI for decisions that matter. For AI whose output is a main basis for decisions with legal or similarly significant effect, OMB Memorandum M-25-21 (April 2025) requires testing before deployment, an impact assessment and ongoing monitoring. An agency that can't see the vendor's code, model or data tests the product by querying it, or by giving the vendor its own test data.
  2. Federal contracts for AI. OMB Memorandum M-25-22 (April 2025) tells agencies to write testing and monitoring of AI into solicitations and contracts. It covers contracts awarded from solicitations issued from late September 2025 on.
  3. Federal purchases of large language models. OMB Memorandum M-26-04 (December 2025) requires an agency buying a large language model to get documentation from the vendor, such as a model or system card, and to write those requirements into the contract.
  4. State IT projects. Kansas requires an independent verification and validation (IV&V) provider on state IT projects that cost more than $10 million (ITEC-2410). Arizona, Maryland, Kentucky and Washington buy IV&V through standing state contracts.
  1. State AI purchasing. In September 2026, Oregon's governor directed the state to seek frontier AI models that have had independent, third-party safety review, and told the state CIO to set criteria for that review (Executive Order 26-26). In March 2026, California's governor directed state agencies to develop certification standards for AI vendors seeking state contracts (Executive Order N-5-26).
  2. State AI policies. Some states already have agencies check a vendor's evidence before they buy. Virginia's AI policy (EA400, June 2026) has state agencies review the results of any testing or red-teaming the vendor ran, including efficacy and bias testing, and work out problems with the vendor before the system goes live. A Texas rule in force since March 18, 2026 requires a written risk assessment, including the system's accuracy measures, before a state agency or local government buys or deploys a heightened-scrutiny AI system. Connecticut's 2024 framework bars agencies from buying an AI solution until its impact has been evaluated.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits a purchase you already run

  1. Before award. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before you sign. See the sample requirement.
  2. Inside an IV&V contract. On a project that already has an IV&V provider, the AI claim can be one task inside that work.
  3. As a small purchase. You can start with a pilot: a first look at one AI product, for a fixed $9,000. Small-purchase limits vary by state, so check yours.

Before you rely on an AI claim

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
