Rules already in place

Hospitals and health plans can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules a hospital, clinic or health plan can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. AI sold as a medical device. FDA reviews AI that meets the legal definition of a medical device. Its guidance on managing AI devices over their life is still a draft. In January 2026 FDA revised its clinical decision support guidance, which leaves more decision support software outside device review.
  2. Decision support inside the health record. Since the end of 2024, certified health record software has had to show users a list of facts about each predictive decision support tool, such as how its model was built and tested. ONC's HTI-5 rule removes that requirement on December 28, 2026. ONC encouraged users to ask their developers for that information.
  3. Discrimination by a decision support tool. Under the 2024 Section 1557 rule, a hospital, clinic or health plan that receives federal health funding must make reasonable efforts to find the decision support tools it uses that take race, color, national origin, sex, age or disability as an input, and to reduce the risk of discrimination from them. That duty has applied since May 2025. A 2025 court order vacated other parts of the rule; HHS says the rest remains in force.
  1. Medicare Advantage plans. CMS lets a plan use an algorithm to help make a coverage decision, but the decision must rest on the patient's own medical history, the doctor's recommendation and clinical notes, not on a larger data set. CMS proposed more AI rules for 2026 and did not adopt them.
  2. State law for health plans. In California since January 2025, only a licensed physician or qualified health professional may deny, delay or change care as not medically necessary, and a plan must review its AI tool's performance from time to time (SB 1120). Washington added in 2026 that a plan's AI policies are open to audit by the insurance commissioner (SB 5395). Colorado's HB 26-1139 sets rules for AI used in coverage review by insurers, pharmacy benefit managers and review firms from January 1, 2027.
  3. Hospital accreditation. In June 2026 the Joint Commission opened a voluntary certification for the responsible use of AI, built on guidance it wrote with the Coalition for Health AI. It looks at a hospital's governance, data, bias and monitoring practices. It does not evaluate or certify AI products.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before you sign. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before you sign. See the sample requirement.
  2. At your AI review committee. When your committee reviews a new tool, a written finding on the vendor's own evidence gives it something specific to weigh. It covers the product, which the Joint Commission certification does not.
  3. For a health plan's review tools. Where state law has you review an AI tool's accuracy, or opens your AI policies to audit, a finding on the vendor's accuracy claim can go in that file.

What a check could look like in healthcare

Made up for this page. No real product or company. Each claim gets the same two findings, signed by the examiner and released by a second reviewer.

The claim

"Our tool finds collapsed lungs on chest X-rays as well as a radiologist does."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Shown to be flawed. Many of the vendor's test X-rays with a collapsed lung also show a chest tube, which is placed after the problem has been found. The test can't tell whether the tool finds the lung or the tube.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: Yes. The vendor's own results, split by whether a tube is in the image, show the tool missed more collapsed lungs than the radiologists did when no tube was present.

Before you rely on an AI claim

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
