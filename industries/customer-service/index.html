Rules already in place

Companies answering customers with AI can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules a company can point to when it asks a customer service AI vendor for evidence.

  1. Claims need a basis. Section 5 of the FTC Act bars unfair or deceptive practices, and the FTC's advertising substantiation policy says advertisers need a reasonable basis for a claim before they make it.
  2. Companion chatbots in California. SB 1119 (2026) requires operators of companion chatbots available to children to submit to independent child-safety audits beginning in 2029.
  3. Colorado. Colorado's HB26-1263 sets disclosure and minor-protection duties for public conversational AI from January 1, 2027, but it excludes tools built mainly for shopping, ordering, returns or customer support.
  1. Payment data. Where a contact center handles card data, the PCI Security Standards Council qualifies independent assessors to check compliance with its standard.
  2. If you serve customers in Europe. The EU AI Act applies to providers outside the EU when the output is used in the EU. Article 50 requires that people be told when they are dealing with an AI system, unless it is obvious.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before you sign. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before you sign. See the sample requirement.
  2. At pilot acceptance. Test the tool on your own agreed questions: accurate answers, telling customers it is AI where required, handing off to a person and protecting customer data.
  3. At renewal or a material change. A new model, new policies or a new channel is a good time to check the claims you rely on again.

A pilot is $9,000, fixed, for a first look at one AI product.

The claim

"Our assistant resolves 80% of customer questions without a person."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Missing. The vendor's materials repeat the number but don't say how it was counted, over what period or on whose questions.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: Not tested. With no evidence to examine, there is nothing to weigh either way.

Before you rely on an AI claim

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
