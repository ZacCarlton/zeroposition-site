Rules already in place

Police departments and courts can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules a police department, court or public safety agency can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. Face recognition in Washington. Under RCW 43.386, an agency that deploys a face recognition service must require the provider to make a way available for independent tests of accuracy and of differences in performance across groups of people. Before using it for decisions with legal or similar effects, the agency must test it in operational conditions.
  2. Face recognition in Colorado. Colorado's SB22-113 sets the same kind of rule for state and local agencies: the provider must support independent testing, and the agency must test the service in operational conditions before it relies on it for decisions with legal or similar effects.
  3. Evidence in federal court. Under Federal Rule of Evidence 702, the side offering expert testimony must show it rests on reliable methods, reliably applied. Rule 901(b)(9) lets a party authenticate the result of a process or system by showing the process produces an accurate result.
  1. A rule for AI evidence is under study. The federal evidence rules committee withdrew its first draft of Rule 707, on machine-generated evidence, after public comment. It voted to study a modified version instead.
  2. How forensic labs are checked. The Justice Department's policy on forensic lab accreditation has an independent body assess the lab's quality system, with method validation and equipment calibration as examples.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before you sign. Make the vendor's evidence for each claim you'll rely on a required submittal, and have it examined before you sign. See the sample requirement.
  2. Before it goes live. Washington and Colorado already require testing in operational conditions before consequential use. A written finding on the vendor's accuracy evidence tells you what the vendor's own testing covered and what your test still has to cover.
  3. Before it reaches a courtroom. A record of the version, settings, test population and error rates is the kind of record a party needs under Rules 702 and 901. It is easier to build before the tool is used in a case than after.

The claim

"Our face matching tool is 99% accurate."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Sound, but short of the claim. The vendor's test holds up, but it used clear, front-facing booking photos. The claim is made for camera stills, which are often blurry or taken at an angle.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: None found inside the claim's scope. That isn't a pass: the evidence doesn't reach the images the tool will actually see.

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
