Rules already in place

Insurers can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules an insurer can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. New York. NYDFS Circular Letter 7 (July 2024) expects insurers using AI in underwriting and pricing to test for unfair discrimination before production, on a regular schedule after that, and after material changes. Where appropriate and available, vendor contracts should give audit rights or audit reports by qualified auditors.
  2. States using the NAIC model bulletin. Pennsylvania's Notice 2024-04, based on the NAIC model bulletin, expects vendor contracts that give audit rights or audit reports by qualified auditors that specifically cover the AI under review, where appropriate and available. The NAIC keeps a map of states that have adopted the bulletin.
  3. Texas. TDI Bulletin B-0003-26 (June 2026) tells regulated companies what information and documentation about their AI the department may request in an investigation or examination.
  1. Colorado. The Division of Insurance's amended Regulation 10-1-1, in force since October 15, 2025, sets governance and risk management rules for life, auto and health insurers that use external consumer data and the models built on it.
  2. A new NAIC review tool. In November 2026, NAIC groups adopted an AI Risk Evaluation Supplement after a pilot run by regulators in 12 states.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. At vendor approval. Ask which model version and which decisions the vendor's audit report covers, and how evidence would reach you and your regulator, before you sign. See the sample requirement.
  2. After a material change. New York expects testing after material changes. A finding on the vendor's evidence for the changed model can sit in the file with that testing.
  3. If you insure AI risk. If a company's risk depends on its AI working as claimed, an underwriter can ask for a finding on those claims before writing the cover. See the page for insurers.

You can start with a pilot: a first look at one AI product, for a fixed $9,000.

The claim

"Our claims model flags fraud equally well for every group of customers."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Sound, but short of the claim. The vendor's test holds up, but it measured accuracy by age and region only. It had no data for other groups the claim covers.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: None found inside the claim's scope. The evidence doesn't reach the groups it didn't measure.

Before you rely on an AI claim

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
