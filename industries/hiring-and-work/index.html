Rules already in place

Employers can already ask for the evidence

These are the rules an employer, staffing agency or HR team can point to when it asks an AI vendor for evidence.

  1. New York City. Under Local Law 144, an employer or employment agency can use an automated employment decision tool for covered hiring or promotion only if it has had an independent bias audit within the past year. The summary must be posted and candidates must get notice.
  2. Federal selection guidelines. Under 29 CFR Part 1607, an employer using a selection procedure with adverse impact needs evidence that it is valid for the job, and the guidelines set standards for relying on someone else's validity study.
  3. California employment rules. California's Civil Rights Council rules on automated decision systems in employment took effect October 1, 2025.
  1. California privacy rules. Under CPPA regulations in force since January 1, 2026, a business that makes automated decision technology available to another business for significant decisions must give that business the facts it has that the other business needs for its own risk assessment.
  2. Colorado. Colorado's SB26-189 requires developers, starting January 1, 2027, to give deployers documentation of a covered tool's intended uses, training data and known limits.
Where it fits

Where an outside check fits work you already do

  1. Before launch. Have the vendor's evidence checked for job-related validity, results by group, and fit with your applicants, not just the vendor's pooled data. See the sample requirement.
  2. When you rely on the vendor's audit. Ask whether the audit's data and positions match your own use before you rely on that report.
  3. Before the year runs out. New York City's audit is good for one year. A material change to the tool or the job is another reason for a fresh look.

The claim

"Our screening tool has no adverse impact on any group."

Finding 1, how far the evidence reaches: Shown to be flawed. The vendor's audit pooled data from other employers and left out applicants the tool screened out before scoring, so the rates it compares are not the tool's real rates.

Finding 2, sound evidence the claim is false: Yes. In the vendor's own audit tables, one group's selection rate is below four-fifths of the highest group's.

Before you rely on an AI claim

You can require an outside check

If your decision rests on what a vendor says its AI product does, you can make an outside check of that claim a condition of the purchase, the policy or the deal. The report comes only to you.
