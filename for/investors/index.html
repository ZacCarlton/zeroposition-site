For investors and acquirers

Before you close, require an outside check of the AI claims

Make it part of diligence. You name the AI claims the deal depends on, an examiner who doesn't work for the company examines the evidence, and the report comes only to you.

What gets checked

The AI claims the deal depends on, and the evidence behind them

AI companies raise money and sell on claims: accuracy, error rates, what the product will and won't do. If the value of the deal depends on one of those claims, that claim is what gets checked.

We examine the evidence for that claim: the test results in the data room and what sits behind them. We don't value the company, and we don't rate the product as a whole.

The Casebook

How to require an outside check

Five things to settle before closing

This is what a deal team needs to write the check into a diligence request or term sheet, and what you get back.

2 · What to ask the company for

  1. The claim, in writing, in the words the company uses to sell it.
  2. Its scope: which version, which uses, which data, and which people or cases it covers.
  3. The evidence behind it: test reports, the data or cases tested, and who ran the tests.
  4. Known limits and error rates, including any measured and not published.
  5. What changed in the product since those tests were run.

3 · Who orders the check

You do. The party that will rely on the claim asks for the check, and the report goes only to the party who asked for the check. The company answers questions in writing and can't edit a finding.

You pay for the check, not the company whose claim is checked. A pilot is $9,000, fixed; other exams are priced in writing after scoping. The fee is set before work starts, and it's the same whatever the finding.

4 · What comes back

A private, signed report on each claim named:

  • The claim as examined, and its scope.
  • Finding 1: how far the evidence reaches, on a five-level scale.
  • Finding 2: whether any sound evidence shows the claim is false, for the uses it covers.
  • What's still open.
  • The ties the examiner and reviewer wrote down before work started.

The examiner signs it, and a second reviewer, independent of the examiner, releases it. See three sample reports.

Finding 1 places the evidence on one of five levels, and your contract or condition sets the level a claim must reach. See the five levels.

5 · When it arrives

The delivery date is set in writing with the scope and the fee, before work starts. Tell us your closing date when you first write, and we'll say whether we can meet it. See the four kinds of exam and how long each takes.

If the evidence falls short

Three choices, if the evidence falls short or isn't shared

The report tells you what the evidence supports. What to do about a gap is your decision. If the evidence isn't shared at all, the claim stays unchecked, and the condition you wrote in is your leverage. These are the usual choices.

WaitHold closing until the company gives evidence that reaches the claim.
Narrow what the deal relies onValue the deal on what the evidence supports, and write that limit into the deal documents.
Accept the risk in writingIf you go ahead, record what the evidence didn't cover, who accepted it, and why.
What it is not

It isn't a valuation

An examination isn't a certification or a valuation, and it isn't a verdict on the company. It's a written finding on the evidence behind the claims you named, for the scope you named. We don't publish it, and we don't name a vendor beside a failure.

In diligence on an AI company?

Send us the claims the deal depends on and the test results you were shown. A blanked-out claim is enough to start.
