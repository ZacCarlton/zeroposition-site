For insurers

Before you cover an AI risk, require an outside check

Make it a condition of the submission or the policy. You name the AI claims the risk depends on, an examiner who doesn't work for the insured or its vendor examines the evidence, and the report comes only to you.

What gets checked

The AI claims the risk depends on, and the evidence behind them

An insured that relies on an AI product is relying on what its vendor says the product does: that it's accurate, that it catches fraud, that it won't give advice it shouldn't. If the risk you're writing depends on one of those claims, that claim is what gets checked.

We examine the evidence for that claim, for the uses the insured describes. We don't examine the insured or the vendor, and we don't rate the product as a whole.

The Casebook

How to require an outside check

Five things to settle before binding

This is what an underwriter needs to make the check a condition of the submission or the policy, and what you get back.

2 · What to ask the insured or its vendor for

  1. The claim, in writing, in the words the vendor uses to sell it.
  2. Its scope: which version, which uses, which data, and which people or cases it covers.
  3. The evidence behind it: test reports, the data or cases tested, and who ran the tests.
  4. Known limits and error rates, including any measured and not published.
  5. What changed in the product since those tests were run.

3 · Who orders the check

You do. The party that will rely on the claim asks for the check, and the report goes only to the party who asked for the check. The insured and its vendor answer questions in writing and can't edit a finding.

You pay for the check, not the company whose claim is checked. The fee is set in writing before work starts, and it's the same whatever the finding.

4 · What comes back

A private, signed report on each claim named:

  • The claim as examined, and its scope.
  • Finding 1: how far the evidence reaches, on a five-level scale.
  • Finding 2: whether any sound evidence, inside the claim's scope, shows the claim is false.
  • What's still open.
  • The ties the examiner and reviewer wrote down before work started.

The examiner signs it, and a second reviewer, independent of the examiner, releases it. See three sample reports.

Finding 1 uses five levels:

  1. Missing: The vendor gave no evidence for this claim.
  2. Shown to be flawed: The evidence has a defect that breaks it.
  3. Not enough to judge: There isn't enough evidence to decide either way.
  4. Sound, but short of the claim: The evidence holds up, but it supports less than the claim says.
  5. Sound, and reaching it: The evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

Your contract or condition sets the level a claim must reach. For example: the claims the decision rests on must reach level 5.

The examiner must have shown practice checking software or systems against stated criteria: for example, an FAA software designee, an independent verification and validation engineer, or a test and evaluation engineer. That seat is being filled.

5 · When it arrives

The delivery date is set in writing with the scope and the fee, before work starts. Tell us your binding date when you first write, and we'll say whether we can meet it.

If the evidence falls short

Three choices, if the evidence falls short or isn't shared

The report tells you what the evidence supports. What to do about a gap is your decision. These are the usual choices.

WaitHold binding until the evidence reaches the claim.
Narrow the coverCover only the uses the evidence reaches, and write that limit into the policy.
Accept the risk in writingIf you go ahead, record what the evidence didn't cover, who accepted it, and why.
What it is not

It isn't a rating

An examination isn't a certification or a rating, and we make no claim about premiums. It's a written finding on the evidence behind the claims you named, for the scope you named. We don't publish it, and we don't name a vendor beside a failure.

Writing a policy on an AI product?

Send us the claim the risk depends on. A public or blanked-out claim is enough to scope the check.
