For buyers and public agencies

Before you rely on an AI vendor's claim, require an outside check

Put the check in the solicitation or contract before award. You name the claims you'll rely on, an examiner who doesn't work for the vendor examines the evidence, and the report comes only to you.

What gets checked

The claims you will rely on, and the evidence behind them

Vendors sell on claims: the product is accurate, it flags fraud, it never gives medical advice. If your decision depends on one of those claims, that claim is what gets checked.

We examine the evidence the vendor gives for that claim, for the uses you name. We don't examine the company, and we don't rate the product as a whole.

How to require an outside check

Five things to settle before award

This is what a purchasing office needs to write the check into a solicitation or contract, and what you get back.

2 · What to ask the vendor for

  1. The claim, in writing, in the words the vendor uses to sell it.
  2. Its scope: which version, which uses, which data, and which people or cases it covers.
  3. The evidence behind it: test reports, the data or cases tested, and who ran the tests.
  4. Known limits and error rates, including any the vendor measured and didn't publish.
  5. What changed in the product since those tests were run.

3 · Who orders the check

You do. The party that will rely on the claim asks for the check, and the report goes only to the party who asked for the check. The vendor answers questions in writing and can't edit a finding.

The fee is set in writing before work starts, and it's the same whatever the finding.

We don't publish the report. Once your agency holds it, your state's public records law governs it.

4 · What comes back

A private, signed report on each claim you named:

  • The claim as examined, and its scope.
  • Finding 1: how far the evidence reaches, on a five-level scale.
  • Finding 2: whether any sound evidence, inside the claim's scope, shows the claim is false.
  • What's still open.
  • The ties the examiner and reviewer wrote down before work started.

The examiner signs it, and a second reviewer, independent of the examiner, releases it. See three sample reports.

Finding 1 uses five levels:

  1. Missing: The vendor gave no evidence for this claim.
  2. Shown to be flawed: The evidence has a defect that breaks it.
  3. Not enough to judge: There isn't enough evidence to decide either way.
  4. Sound, but short of the claim: The evidence holds up, but it supports less than the claim says.
  5. Sound, and reaching it: The evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

Your contract or condition sets the level a claim must reach. For example: the claims the decision rests on must reach level 5.

The examiner must have shown practice checking software or systems against stated criteria: for example, an FAA software designee, an independent verification and validation engineer, or a test and evaluation engineer. That seat is being filled.

5 · When it arrives

The delivery date is set in writing with the scope and the fee, before work starts. Tell us your award date when you first write, and we'll say whether we can meet it.

If the evidence falls short

Three choices, if the evidence falls short or the vendor won't share it

The report tells you what the evidence supports. What to do about a gap is your decision. These are the usual choices.

WaitHold the award, or the go-live date, until the vendor gives evidence that reaches the claim.
Narrow the useUse the product only where the evidence reaches, and write that limit into the contract.
Accept the risk in writingIf you go ahead, record what the evidence didn't cover, who accepted it, and why.
What it is not

It isn't a certification

An examination isn't a certification, and it isn't a verdict on the vendor. It's a written finding on the evidence behind the claims you named, for the scope you named. We don't publish it, and we don't name a vendor beside a failure.

Writing a solicitation, or about to award?

Send us the claim you'd rely on. A public or blanked-out claim is enough to scope the check.
