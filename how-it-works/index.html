How it works

What an examination is, and what you get back

We examine the evidence behind a claim you plan to rely on, and send you a signed, private report with two findings on it.

Finding 1: How far does the evidence reach?

We read the vendor's evidence for the claim and place it on one of five levels. Only the fifth means the evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

  1. 1MissingThe vendor gave no evidence for this claim.
  2. 2Shown to be flawedThe evidence has a defect that breaks it.
  3. 3Not enough to judgeThere isn't enough evidence to decide either way.
  4. 4Sound, but short of the claimThe evidence holds up, but it supports less than the claim says.
  5. 5Sound, and reaching itThe evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

Finding 2: Does any evidence show the claim is false?

We look for sound evidence that the claim is false, for the uses the claim covers. Finding none isn't a pass. It means we looked and found nothing that breaks the claim.

What the report contains

  • The claim as examined, in the vendor's words, and the scope you named.
  • Finding 1, with its level and the reasons.
  • Finding 2, with what was found or a plain statement that nothing was.
  • What's still open.
  • The ties the examiner and the second reviewer wrote down, to the vendor, to you and to the deal, before work started.

The examiner signs it. The second reviewer releases it. It goes only to the party who asked for the check.

What it can't tell you

It isn't a certification, and it isn't a verdict on the company or on its other claims. It's a written finding on the vendor's evidence, and on what's still open, for the scope you named.

What you bring

  • The claim, in the vendor's words.
  • What you plan to use the product for.
  • Any evidence the vendor already gave you.
  • A contact at the vendor, if you have one.

A public or blanked-out claim is enough to scope the work.

Independence

The rules that keep the check honest

Who wrote the list?

Not the vendor. Two lists of checks, built apart.

Do we get paid more for a pass?

No. Every fee is set before the work starts, and a refusal pays the same.

Who can overrule the result?

Not me. A signer who wrote none of the checks applies the scoring rules, and I can't overrule a refusal.

What can the vendor do?

Answer written questions. The vendor can't edit a finding.

Four kinds of exam

From a two-week pilot to a high-stakes exam

Every kind gives the same two findings on each claim, signed by the examiner and released by a second reviewer. They differ in how much of the product is covered and how far we go to test it.

Pilot

A first, bounded look at one AI product in one part of your business.

What it covers
The vendor's evidence for a small number of claims, read against public sources. No interviews.
What you get
The two findings on each claim, and what is still open.
How long
2 weeks
Price
$9,000, fixed

Standard

For one AI system you are buying or relying on.

What it covers
Up to five claims. We examine the vendor's evidence and talk with the people who built and run the product.
What you get
The two findings on each claim, what is still open, and the ties the examiner and reviewer declared.
How long
4 weeks
Price
Priced in writing after scoping

Deep dive

For when the evidence alone may not settle the claim.

What it covers
Everything in a standard exam, across more of the product, plus our own tests where the vendor's evidence doesn't reach the claim.
What you get
The standard report, with our test results beside the vendor's.
How long
About 8 weeks. Faster on request: we can add engineers to meet your date.
Price
Priced in writing after scoping

High-stakes

For mission-critical work, autonomous agents, defense, nuclear, space and robotics.

What it covers
Scoped for each job, with the team sized to the risk.
What you get
The deep-dive report, with the scope and team set out in writing before work starts.
How long
Set for each job.
Price
Priced in writing after scoping

The party who asks for the check pays for it, never the company whose claim is checked. We agree the scope, the fee and the delivery date in writing before work starts, and the fee doesn't change with the finding. If you have an award, binding or closing date, tell us when you first write.
