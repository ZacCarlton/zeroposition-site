How it works

What an examination is, and what you get back

We examine the evidence behind a claim you plan to rely on, and send you a signed, private report with two findings on it.

Finding 1: How far does the evidence reach?

We read the vendor's evidence for the claim and place it on one of five levels. Only the fifth means the evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

  1. 1MissingThe vendor gave no evidence for this claim.
  2. 2Shown to be flawedThe evidence has a defect that breaks it.
  3. 3Not enough to judgeThere isn't enough evidence to decide either way.
  4. 4Sound, but short of the claimThe evidence holds up, but it supports less than the claim says.
  5. 5Sound, and reaching itThe evidence holds up and supports the whole claim.

Finding 2: Does any evidence show the claim is false?

We look for sound evidence, inside the claim's scope, that the claim is false. Finding none isn't a pass. It means we looked and found nothing that breaks the claim.

What the report contains

  • The claim as examined, in the vendor's words, and the scope you named.
  • Finding 1, with its level and the reasons.
  • Finding 2, with what was found or a plain statement that nothing was.
  • What's still open.
  • The ties the examiner and the second reviewer wrote down, to the vendor, to you and to the deal, before work started.

The examiner signs it. The second reviewer releases it. It goes only to the party who asked for the check.

What it can't tell you

It isn't a certification, and it isn't a verdict on the company or on its other claims. It's a written finding on the vendor's evidence, and on what's still open, for the scope you named.

Our own method has not finished its blind test, so there is no measured error rate for it yet. See where that stands.

What you bring

  • The claim, in the vendor's words.
  • What you plan to use the product for.
  • Any evidence the vendor already gave you.
  • A contact at the vendor, if you have one.

A public or blanked-out claim is enough to scope the work.

Independence

The rules that keep the check honest

Who wrote the list?

Not the vendor. Two lists of checks, built apart.

Do we get paid more for a pass?

No. Every fee is set before the work starts, and a refusal pays the same.

Who can overrule the result?

Not me. A signer who wrote none of the checks applies the scoring rules, and I can't overrule a refusal.

What can the vendor do?

Answer written questions. The vendor can't edit a finding.

Timing and terms

Scope, fee and date are set before work starts

We agree the scope, the fee and the delivery date in writing before the examination starts. The fee doesn't change with the finding. If you have a deadline, an award date or a closing date, tell us when you first write, and we'll say whether we can meet it.

Have a vendor claim you need to rely on?

Send it to us. A public or blanked-out claim is enough to start, and we'll tell you what the check would cover.
