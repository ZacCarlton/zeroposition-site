Independent examination of the evidence behind AI claims

The vendor says it works. Who checked?

For anyone who has to rely on an AI vendor's claim and answer for it: public agencies buying AI, hospitals, insurers, and investors in AI deals.

When an AI vendor says its product is safe or accurate, we examine the evidence behind that one claim, as examiners who don't work for the vendor, and sign what it does and doesn't show. Today most buyers, insurers and investors have the vendor's own test reports to go on, and a report can look convincing while testing the wrong thing.

The examination

What you get: two findings on each claim, signed

You name the claims your decision depends on: as an insurer pricing coverage, a company buying an AI product, or an investor in a deal. For each claim, the report gives two findings. The first rates the vendor's evidence. The second says whether we found evidence that the claim is false.

What it doesn't do+

It doesn't test the product. It doesn't say whether the product works, is safe, is good, or will perform once it's deployed. There's no score, grade, tier or certification. It says nothing about claims you didn't name, beyond listing them. The examiner doesn't fix anything.

What it can't tell you+
  • It can't confirm the records are real beyond checking them for consistency.
  • It can't confirm the version tested is the one you'd receive.
  • Finding no problems in a sample says nothing about the parts that weren't sampled.
What you bring+

The claims you rely on, and the vendor's evidence for each, through you and under your NDA. The examiner needs no source code, running copy of the product, or credentials. If a check needs one of those, the report says unknown and names what it needs.

How it works

Four steps, from your claim to a signed report

  1. You send the claimThe claim you would rely on, in the vendor's words, and what you plan to use the product for. A public or blanked-out claim is enough to start.
  2. We agree scope, fee and dateIn writing, before work starts. The fee is the same whatever the finding.
  3. The evidence is examinedThe examiner asks the vendor for its evidence and written answers. The vendor can't edit a finding.
  4. You get a signed, private reportTwo findings on each claim, what's still open, and the scope. The report goes only to the party who asked for the check.
