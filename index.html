01 · The examination

What you get: two findings on each claim, signed

You name the claims your decision depends on: as an insurer pricing coverage, a company buying an AI product, or an investor in a deal. For each claim, the report gives two findings. The first rates the vendor's evidence. The second says whether we found evidence that the claim is false.

ExaminerSigned Second reviewerReleased No score, grade, tier or certification. A written finding on the evidence, for the scope you named.
What it doesn't do+

It doesn't test the product. It doesn't say whether the product works, is safe, is good, or will perform once it's deployed. There's no score, grade, tier or certification. It says nothing about claims you didn't name, beyond listing them. The examiner doesn't fix anything.

What it can't tell you+
  • It can't confirm the records are real beyond checking them for consistency.
  • It can't confirm the version tested is the one you'd receive.
  • Zero problems found in a sample means zero found in that sample.
What you bring+

The claims you rely on, and the vendor's evidence for each, through you and under your NDA. The examiner needs no source code, running copy of the product, or credentials. If a check needs one of those, the report says unknown and names what it needs.

02 · Method and blind test

We test our method first, in public

Before we sell an examination, we test the method blind. Eight cases can't give a general error rate. They show whether the method catches what it's built to catch. More rounds come after that.

  1. Step 1 A red team builds the cases Eight made-up vendor cases: two clean, and six with planted faults.
  2. Step 2 Two inspectors work them apart A human inspector and an AI inspector work the same cases, apart.
  3. Step 3 An outside examiner repeats it An outside examiner repeats the round without the key.
  4. Step 4 Everything is released Everything from the round is released, pass or fail.

I ask three questions of any check, starting with my own

Who wrote the list?

Not the vendor. Two lists, built apart. A red team plants problems in made-up evidence files, some outside both lists.

Do I pay more for a pass?

No. Every fee is set before the work starts, and a refusal pays the same.

Who can overrule the result?

Not me. A signer who wrote none of it applies the scoring rules. I can't overrule a refusal.

03 · How mature industries do it

How mature fields check a claim

In these fields, the company being checked doesn't write the standard by itself or decide who's qualified to check against it. Forensic science went a step further: it measured how often its own examiners get it wrong.

The lead example · fingerprints

A measured error rate, and a second examiner who catches it

In 2011 the FBI and Noblis tested 169 experienced examiners on prints where the right answer was known, and published how often they were wrong.

In 2016 the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology found fingerprint analysis to be a valid method, called its false positive rate substantial, and said juries should be told about it.

Sources: Ulery, Hicklin, Buscaglia and Roberts, PNAS, 2011 · PCAST, 2016

0.1%false positives: a match called between prints from different people
7.5%false negatives: a real match missed
Inferred · my comparison

That's what I want for AI claims: a written method, evidence against the claim, a known error rate, and a second examiner who can catch the first.

The claim. This print came from this person.

Who checks it. A latent print examiner, working to a written method (ACE-V).

What keeps it honest. A second examiner verifies identifications. Accredited labs are assessed by an outside body, and examiners take regular proficiency tests. Error rates are measured in published studies.

Sources: PCAST, 2016: ACE-V and verification · ANAB forensic lab accreditation · ANAB AR 3125: proficiency testing · Ulery and others, PNAS, 2011: error rates

The claim. This design meets the airworthiness rules.

Who checks it. An FAA Designated Engineering Representative, who signs a statement of compliance on FAA Form 8110-3.

What keeps it honest. The FAA appoints its designees and oversees their work.

Sources: FAA Order 8110.37G, DER Handbook · FAA: compliance findings on Form 8110-3

The claim. This product is safe to use at work.

Who checks it. A Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory, testing against published safety standards.

What keeps it honest. OSHA decides which labs are recognized, and audits them.

Sources: 29 CFR 1910.7: NRTL requirements · OSHA NRTL program

The claim. These financial statements are fairly stated.

Who checks it. An outside audit firm registered with the PCAOB.

What keeps it honest. The PCAOB inspects completed audits. In 2025, 23% of the audits it reviewed had not gathered enough evidence to support the opinion.

Sources: PCAOB, Staff Update on 2025 Inspections, October 22, 2026

The claim. This building was built to code.

Who checks it. A building inspector who works for the city, not the builder.

What keeps it honest. Inspections at set stages before the work is covered, and no certificate of occupancy until the inspector signs off. Many states license inspectors or require ICC certification; the rules vary by state.

Sources: ICC: inspections and certificates of occupancy · ICC Residential Building Inspector (B1) certification · Utah: inspector licenses require ICC certification

The claim. This product is safe, or accurate.

Who checks it. Most often, the vendor's own test reports.

What keeps it honest. Standards are starting to form. Certification bodies for ISO/IEC 42001 are accredited, but that standard certifies how a company manages AI, not whether a product claim holds up.

Sources: ANAB: ISO/IEC 42001 accreditation for certification bodies · ANSI: ISO/IEC 42001 does not certify products or services

Open a row to see its sources. "Not found" means I looked for a published error rate and didn't find one.

Inferred · my comparison as a contractor

I can't hand a family the keys until an inspector who doesn't work for me signs off. Who does that before you rely on an AI product?

What exists today

The market today offers deal diligence, certification of how a company manages AI (ISO/IEC 42001), checks the vendor pays for, and checks run by an insurer that also sells the coverage. I haven't found a published method, run by examiners who don't work for the vendor, that checks whether the evidence behind a specific AI claim holds up, tested blind, repeated by an outside examiner, with an error rate.

On October 14, Kansas sued OpenAI under its Consumer Protection Act, alleging its safety claims had no reasonable basis when they were made. Seven other states filed the same day. No court has ruled. News coverage: KCTV5, October 14, 2026; HPPR, October 16, 2026.
